---
title: "Shoe sizing past size 13"
layout: prose
---

Sizes above 13 are where conversion charts start disagreeing with each other. The
tables below are the common conventions. **Treat them as a starting point, not a
guarantee** — the actual last a brand uses matters more than the number printed
inside the shoe.

## US men's, UK and EU

| US men's | UK | EU (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | 12 | 47 |
| 14 | 13 | 48 |
| 15 | 14 | 49 |
| 16 | 15 | 50 |
| 17 | 16 | 51 |
| 18 | 17 | 52 |

The UK number is normally one below the US men's number. The EU number is
approximate and rounds differently between makers — a half-size gap in US terms
often has no EU equivalent at all, so brands round in whichever direction suits
their last.

## Why the same size fits differently

- **Length is not the whole story.** Two shoes marked 15 can differ in
  instep height, toe box depth and heel volume. Length is the only dimension
  the number describes.
- **Scaling gets rough at the top of the range.** Many patterns are graded up
  from a mid-size sample. The further a size sits from that sample, the more the
  proportions drift.
- **Half sizes thin out.** Above 14, some makers stop offering half sizes
  entirely, which pushes people into rounding up.
- **Category changes the fit.** Running shoes are usually cut closer than work
  boots in the same nominal size.

## Practical reading

If a size is at the very top of a brand's range, it is more likely to be a scaled
pattern than a purpose-built one. Where a brand offers the same size in more than
one width, the wider option is often the better fit even for a normal-width foot,
because the extra volume compensates for a shallow toe box.
