---
title: "What shoe width letters mean"
layout: prose
---

Width is the measurement most often ignored and most often responsible for a shoe
that does not fit. It describes girth around the ball of the foot, not length.

## US men's width designations

| Marking | Also written | Common name |
|---|---|---|
| B | — | Narrow |
| D | M, Medium | Standard |
| 2E | EE, W | Wide |
| 4E | EEEE, XW | Extra wide |
| 6E | EEEEEE | Extra extra wide |

**D is the default.** A shoe with no width marking is almost always D.

Each step up adds roughly an eighth to a quarter inch of girth, depending on the
maker — there is no enforced standard, so a 2E from one brand can measure closer
to another brand's D.

## Where it matters most

- **Width availability collapses at large sizes.** Plenty of brands make a size
  15; far fewer make a size 15 in 4E. That narrowing is the single hardest part
  of shopping at the top of the range.
- **Length gets substituted for width.** Going up a full size to gain room is a
  common workaround and a poor one: it adds length the foot does not need, which
  moves the flex point of the shoe away from where the foot actually bends.
- **The marking may cover a range.** Some makers publish one width but build to a
  tolerance wide enough to cover two.

## Practical reading

If a shoe is available in several widths, that usually signals the brand builds a
separate last per width rather than stretching one pattern. That is a better sign
for fit than the width letter on its own.
